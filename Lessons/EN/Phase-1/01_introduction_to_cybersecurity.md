# Stage 1: Introduction to Cybersecurity

## What Is Cybersecurity?

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting **digital systems**, the **information** they hold, and the **people** who use them from harm.

Think of it like the locks, alarms, security cameras, and guards that protect a physical building — except the “building” is made of computers, networks, software, and data.

The goal is simple to state but complex to achieve:

> Keep the right people able to use systems and data when they need them, while keeping the wrong people out and preventing accidental damage.

## Why Does Cybersecurity Exist?

Three big reasons:

1. **We store valuable things digitally**  
   Money, personal photos, medical records, company secrets, government information — almost everything important now exists as data.

2. **Digital systems are connected**  
   Most computers talk to other computers over networks (especially the internet). Connection creates convenience *and* risk. A weak point in one place can affect many others.

3. **People and organizations depend on these systems**  
   Hospitals, banks, power grids, transportation, and ordinary daily life all rely on working technology. When those systems fail or are misused, real-world harm follows.

## A Simple Everyday Analogy

Imagine you own a house:

- You want family and invited guests to enter easily.
- You want strangers kept out.
- You want the house to remain usable even if someone tries to break a window.
- You want important papers inside to stay private and undamaged.

Cybersecurity applies the same ideas to computers, phones, cloud services, and data.

## Key Terms You Will See Everywhere

| Term | Plain-language meaning |
|------|------------------------|
| **Asset** | Anything worth protecting (data, a computer, a service, reputation) |
| **Threat** | Something that could cause harm to an asset |
| **Vulnerability** | A weakness that a threat could exploit |
| **Risk** | The chance that a threat will successfully harm an asset, combined with how bad the impact would be |
| **Control / Countermeasure** | A safeguard put in place to reduce risk (a lock, a password policy, encryption, training, etc.) |

These four ideas — asset, threat, vulnerability, risk — form the basic vocabulary of the entire field. We will expand on them in later stages.

## What Cybersecurity Is *Not*

- It is not only about “hackers in hoodies.” Most security work is about design, policy, monitoring, and reducing everyday mistakes.
- It is not a single product you can buy. It is an ongoing process involving technology, people, and procedures.
- It is not the same as “privacy,” although the two overlap heavily. Privacy is about controlling who can see personal information; cybersecurity is the broader practice of protecting systems and data from many kinds of harm.

## The Big Picture

Cybersecurity professionals spend their time answering questions such as:

- What are we trying to protect?
- What could go wrong?
- How likely is it, and how bad would it be?
- What can we reasonably do to reduce the danger?
- How do we know our protections are still working?

The rest of this series gives you the foundational ideas needed to understand those questions at a professional level.
